Class 10 · Science
CHAPTER 01

Chemical Reactions and Equations

Science

Quick revision notes for Class 10 students (RBSE / CBSE aligned basics).

1. Chemical Reaction

A process in which one or more substances (reactants) change into new substances (products) with different properties is called a chemical reaction.

Common observations: change in colour, change in temperature, evolution of gas, or formation of a precipitate.

2. Chemical Equation

A chemical equation represents a chemical reaction using symbols and formulae. A balanced equation has an equal number of atoms of each element on both sides.

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

3. Types of Chemical Reactions

Combination: Two or more substances combine to form one product.

CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂

Decomposition: One compound breaks into simpler substances.

CaCO₃ --heat→ CaO + CO₂

Displacement: A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element.

Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

Double displacement: Ions are exchanged between two compounds.

Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl

4. Oxidation and Reduction

  • Oxidation: Addition of oxygen or removal of hydrogen.
  • Reduction: Removal of oxygen or addition of hydrogen.
  • Oxidation and reduction occur together in a redox reaction.

5. Exothermic and Endothermic Reactions

  • Exothermic: Releases heat energy, e.g. combustion.
  • Endothermic: Absorbs energy, e.g. thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate.

6. Corrosion and Rancidity

  • Corrosion: Gradual damage of metals due to air, moisture or chemicals. Prevention includes painting, oiling, galvanising and alloying.
  • Rancidity: Oxidation of fats and oils that causes unpleasant smell and taste. It can be slowed by airtight storage, refrigeration and antioxidants.

Important Questions

  1. What is a balanced chemical equation? Why should an equation be balanced?
  2. Explain combination and decomposition reactions with examples.
  3. What is a displacement reaction? Give one example.
  4. Differentiate between oxidation and reduction.
  5. What are corrosion and rancidity? How can they be prevented?
Remember: The total number of atoms of each element remains the same before and after a chemical reaction.
